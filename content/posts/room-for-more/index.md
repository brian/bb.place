<p><em>Room</em> is a word whose diminutive stature and common presence mask its central place in so many of our stories. We're told we have room for improvement and we ask for a room with a view. Not leaving your room for a week generates giggles in one context and concern in another. Attaching a tiny word like <em>dorm</em> to the beginning brings to mind faces and stories and clever uses of space. Placing <em>mate</em> at the end recalls treasured friends, dalliances regretted or fondly remembered, and arguments over missing leftovers. Boardrooms, bedrooms, living rooms — all conjure up memories, plot lines, and  arguments.</p><p>I'm writing this from my new room, an office filled with small treasures—treasured gifts, <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisenaire_rods">Cuisenaire rods</a>, prints and album covers, and a kaleidoscope.</p><p>There's something different about a room of your own, where inspirations and little bursts of happiness are everywhere you turn. Then again, whenever I find myself marveling at this haven, I look over at the empty couch and think of people I wish were sitting on it. Every room should leave room for others.</p>
