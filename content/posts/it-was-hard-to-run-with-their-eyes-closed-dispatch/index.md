<!--kg-card-begin: markdown--><p><em>In an old house in Paris that was covered with vines<br>
Lived twelve little girls in two straight lines.<br>
In two straight lines, they broke their bread,<br>
and brushed their teeth, and went to bed.<br>
They smiled at the good, and frowned at the bad,<br>
and sometimes they were very sad.</em></p>
<!--kg-card-end: markdown--><p>Last week's horrific events in Newtown, Connecticut have been swirling in my mind and heart. I don't think any of us know how to process such a tragedy. Everything seems trivial in the face of such overwhelming sorrow.</p><p>For some reason, my mind keeps drifting to <em>Madeline</em> by Ludwig Bemelmans, one of my favorite children's books. It's the wonderful tale of a school for young girls, including the plucky Madeline and her teacher and protector, Miss Clavel. It's Miss Clavel's keen ability to sense trouble that I return to again and again.</p><!--kg-card-begin: markdown--><p><em>In the middle of the night, Miss Clavel turned on her light.<br>
And she said, &quot;Something is not right.”</em></p>
<!--kg-card-end: markdown--><p><em>Something is not right.</em> We know that now. We likely knew it before, but we were able to lose ourselves in the day-to-day, rushing to the next place and next thing, and not face it.</p><p>There are so many stories of Miss Clavel in Newtown, Connecticut; protectors who fought and shielded, comforted and quieted. <a href="http://www.nytimes.com/2012/12/18/nyregion/amid-the-whiz-of-bullets-seeking-comfort-in-song.html?smid=tw-share">This New York Times story</a> is unbelievably gripping. There's a line in that piece about the children at Sandy Hook Elementary School that I will never forget: "...it was hard to run with their eyes closed."</p><p>We know, little ones. We know because we've been doing it for a long time.</p><p>I can't offer a solution. I only know that things will get better through thousands of small decisions by every one of us, and maybe a few big ones, too. For the children in our midst, and their protectors, let's stop running, and open our eyes.</p><p>The Times has <a href="http://cityroom.blogs.nytimes.com/how-to-help-in-newtown-conn/">listed ways we can help</a>.</p><p>We continue on with heavy hearts, and prayers for the many who are hurting.</p><h3 id="uncommon-reads">Uncommon reads</h3><p><a href="https://bb.place/uncommon/story/always-tinged-with-the-hope-of-next-time">Last week's dispatch</a> asked, <em>What book or article did you read this year that you wish everyone could read?</em></p><p><a href="https://twitter.com/amcmanus">Adam</a> wrote:</p><blockquote><a href="http://www.robinsloan.com/penumbra/">Mr. Penumbra's 24-Hour Bookstore</a>.
