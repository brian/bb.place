<p>With nephews around last week, I had the chance to bring out a favorite possession, a rather epic kaleidoscope. It was one of my father's few indulgent purchases. An eclectic toy shop prominently displayed it to entice visitors and start conversations. When he found out that everyone loved it, but no one had yet been willing to pay the substantial price, he mischievously decided to be the first.</p><p>This kaleidoscope has a separate glass sphere filled with liquid that sits at the end of the brass tube. You can turn the sphere or shine a light through it. A small twist changes the view entirely.</p><p>I'm fascinated by shifts in perspective. Is the restaurant empty or intimate? Are we full of ideas or unable to concentrate? Is it <a href="http://randsinrepose.com/archives/r-i-p-things/">stagnation</a> or deliberate craftsmanship? Are the holidays brimming or overwhelming?</p><p>New perspectives often arrive at unexpected times and by surprising means; an off-hand remark by a friend, a photo or lyric, a memento from a different time, a new route. Turn the sphere, shine a light, and relish the changing colors and patterns.</p><h3 id="prompted">Prompted</h3><p><a href="https://bb.place/uncommon/story/the-fruits-of-folks-helping-each-other">Last week's dispatch</a> asked, <em>Where are you a regular?</em> I want to visit them all.</p><p>Cal wrote:</p><blockquote>I'm a regular at a speakeasy just a hop and a skip from my office.
