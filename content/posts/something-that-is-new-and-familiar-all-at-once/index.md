<p>I hope your week is going swimmingly well or at least includes a quick dip in a pool. Or maybe a double dip (more on that shortly).</p><p>I thought you might enjoy a little story about how the ridiculous name, Uncommon In Common dot com, came to be. I mentioned the dream of a slow web community to <a href="https://twitter.com/robinsloan">Robin Sloan</a>, who recently created <a href="http://www.robinsloan.com/fish/">Fish: a tap essay</a> (it’s wonderful, if you haven’t read it yet).</p><p>He was intrigued by the community idea, but thought the name, Toyed With, didn’t quite fit. I mentioned other possibilities and then for the fun of it, included Uncommon In Common dot com. I had always loved it and laughed at it and didn’t know which reaction to trust. His response was completely unexpected.</p><blockquote>For what it’s worth I think uncommonincommon is pretty great. I just think it has a nice bit of poetry to it. And its length is almost a statement: "Bah, Tumblr! Bah Pinterest! Bah Zynga and Flarbo! You can keep your two-syllable made-up words. This site’s name has eight syllables so just slowww down."</blockquote><p>I couldn’t stop smiling. This started conversations with many others and a few days later, we had a new name. Something amazing happens when someone doesn’t laugh at your laughable idea.</p><h3 id="news-and-such">News and such</h3><p>Every day, through email threads, coffee shop conversations, and Basecamp<br>discussions, Uncommon becomes a little bit more defined. So many of you have taken the time to share your ideas and wishes, and offer thoughtful feedback. It’s enormously helpful.</p><p>Work is continuing on a simple one-page site to serve as a temporary home. We’re also exploring the best ways to reflect the ethos of Uncommon through art and design. I’ll share more as the pieces fall into place. The pieces are only a beginning, though. All of us, together, will determine what Uncommon becomes.</p><h3 id="your-favorites">Your favorites</h3><blockquote>Making errors is the essence of creativity. Better accidents are what make us go forward. I’ve been stuck in a rut of not trying recently, because I’m afraid of errors. But errors are good, if you make enough errors you’re bound to discover something. — <a href="https://twitter.com/joachimesque">Joachim</a> on a favorite recent read, this terrific <a href="http://www.thefirst10000.com/2012/06/joerg-colbergs-photography-after-photography-considered-or-a-response-to-a-provocation/">essay on photography</a></blockquote><blockquote>One of my favorite things is something that is new and familiar all at once—like a vase of fresh flowers in a color or variety I’ve never had before, or ice cream in a surprising flavor. — <a href="https://twitter.com/lmsanchez">Lisa</a></blockquote><h3 id="uncommon-reads">Uncommon reads</h3><p><a href="http://notes.torrez.org/2012/07/we-met-on-the-internet.html">we met on the internet</a> by Andre Torrez</p><blockquote>It’s taking me a while, but I feel like I am getting closer figuring out how to let the parade march by and go happily along my way.</blockquote><p><a href="http://caterina.net/2011/03/15/fomo-and-social-media/">FOMO and Social Media</a> by Caterina Fake in March of last year, but well worth another look</p><blockquote>There is true meaning in social media—real connections, real friendships, devotion, humor, sacrifice, joy, depth, love.
