<p>My Gmail address is simple enough that I regularly get email meant for someone else. I usually ignore it, but sometimes the content seems important enough that I let the sender know. My favorite was when I wrote, "You have the wrong address." and the person replied, "Sorry, what address do you want me to use?"</p><p>This year offered a new wrinkle. It seems there is a Reverend Brian Bailey in England and I started receiving email addressed to him. There was nothing typical or unimportant about these messages. They were emails from family members of someone recently deceased. The person (usually a son or daughter) was offering stories and tidbits for the Reverend to use in the upcoming funeral.</p><p>The stories were beautiful and sad, touching and funny, as you would expect. I remember one lovely line, "May her soul continue to dance."</p><p>It felt enormously awkward to receive such personal messages or to interject myself, a stranger in another country, into such a significant moment. I had to let them know they had sent the message to the wrong person, though, and so replied as kindly as possible.</p><p>This left some people confused. In the middle of the stress and distractions of that moment, no one wants to track down a problem with an email address. So, I gleaned bits of information here and there and after additional research, I located Rev. Brian Bailey of Scunthorpe along with his email address.</p><p>Now, when the emails arrive, my reply is more helpful. I tell them I'm sorry for their loss and include the correct email address. They are often grateful to have one less thing to think about.</p><p>These brief, accidental exchanges with people I will never meet have meant a lot to me. What a shift in perspective a single misdirected email can provide. Suddenly, whatever I'm currently obsessing over looks quite different. Also, any encounter with such heartfelt honesty is worthwhile. I am truly grateful for these mistakes.</p><p>My favorite mistake this year happened on Twitter, a story <a href="https://twitter.com/ResonanceGlobal">Radhika</a> mentioned in a <a href="/uncommon/story/the-fruits-of-folks-helping-each-other">previous dispatch</a>. Radhika <a href="https://twitter.com/bb/statuses/389516027505041409">accidentally replied to me</a>, which happens all the time, but she was the first person who sent a follow-up reply.</p><p>That simple kindness led me to listen to her <a href="http://resonancemusicproject.com/home/">wonderful music</a> and she started reading about Uncommon. Radhika has since become an integral part of this community and may play a part in a future gathering, all from a random mistake.</p><p>When I think about the year ahead, I hope to be more open to mistakes and surprising moments of serendipity.</p><h3 id="prompted">Prompted</h3><p><a href="/uncommon/story/artifacts-that-tie-us-together">Last week's dispatch</a> asked, <em>What would you like more or less of in the new year?</em></p><p><a href="http://www.lot23.com/">Jon</a> wrote:</p><blockquote>I want more less.
