<p>As the online home for Uncommon slowly takes shape, many knotty questions surface. Features and interactions are designed and built, and what we want Uncommon to be often collides with how we've learned to do things online. A recent example is what should the experience of visiting Uncommon a few days or weeks after your last visit be?</p><p>We all know how this is supposed to work. You should be greeted by visual cues that you are terribly behind, numbers in red circles that tell you just how much you've missed. The goal is to instill a fear of missing out, lest you make the mistake of not visiting frequently enough again.</p><p>At the core of Uncommon is a very different view.</p><p>Uncommon should be there for you on your terms. We want every experience to be enriching and unhurried regardless of when the last visit was. We will never send notifications to your phone or display the number of prompts awaiting your replay. There are more than enough obligations and demands on our attention.</p><p>When I have the chance to talk with someone about Uncommon, a funny sense of guilt will sometimes find its way into the conversation. "I'm so far behind on the dispatches! I need to catch up." "I keep meaning to reply, but I'm always so late I'm too embarrassed to send it."</p><p>I love to explain that there are no apologies in Uncommon, no late or falling behind.</p><p>This isn't a competing priority, but a supportive community.
