<p><em>With this edition of the dispatch, we're starting something new: Each quarter, we’ll introduce a seasonal theme. As our community takes root, we have the opportunity to explore an idea together in more depth. For the last quarter of 2014, our theme is <strong>Doors</strong>.</em></p><p>Few people have played a bigger part in the story of this community than <a href="http://seriouspony.com/">Kathy Sierra</a>. From the start, Kathy's enthusiasm and support made all the difference. When I was unsure whether the idea was worth pursuing, she convinced me it was. Over the next few months, she tolerated my lengthy emails and provided fountains of wisdom in reply, then wrote a <a href="/uncommon/story/the-api-of-you">thought-provoking essay</a> for our founding members. And when she joined our community, she insisted on paying despite her endless contributions. I know she is a friend and hero to many of you as well.</p><p>This story would be more remarkable if it wasn't so common. Kathy has long championed each person's heroic journey. Anyone who has had the privilege of attending one of <a href="http://vimeo.com/106659444">her</a> <a href="http://vimeo.com/54469442">amazing</a> <a href="http://vimeo.com/81625882">talks</a> leaves believing they can make things better. Her groundbreaking <a href="http://www.amazon.com/Kathy-Sierra/e/B001H6U55G/">book series</a> taught Java to millions of developers (and taught authors a new way to approach writing). Through her <a href="http://headrush.typepad.com/">Creating Passionate Users blog</a> and work with companies large and small, she has been a persistent crusader for products and environments that respect and empower people.</p><p>At its heart, her message is simple and profound: it's not about your app or book (or whatever else), it's about <em>what people do as a result of your app or book</em>.</p><p>Always supportive, always generous with her time, Kathy has opened doors for authors <em>and</em> readers, developers <em>and</em> users. Through her example, she has demonstrated the absurdity of technology's gender imbalance.</p><p>For all of this, she has paid a price. Kathy has been the subject of trolls and attacks in ways none of us can stomach. This abuse has made it nearly impossible for her to be present online. Last July she returned to Twitter after many years away, but recently stepped away again. <a href="http://seriouspony.com/trouble-at-the-koolaid-point">Her account of the past ten years</a> is disturbing and infuriating.</p><p>It's unacceptable when any one of us has to decide whether expressing ourselves online is worth the cost of threats, disparagement, bullying, and the loss of personal privacy. We must do better. Uncommon gives me hope that a community of people can care about each other as we share with wonder the best parts of the world around us.</p><p>If you'd like to join us in thanking Kathy for her continuous inspiration, wisdom, and example, <a href="/uncommon/story/hello">please send</a> your stories and kind words. We’ll gather our collective gratitude and pass it along.</p><p>Thanks for opening so many doors for all of us, Kathy. We stand beside you. Your insight, humor, and pony photos are forever welcomed here.</p><h3 id="prompted">Prompted</h3><p><a href="/uncommon/story/seconds-of-unfiltered-inspiration">Last week's dispatch</a> asked, <em>Do you have a favorite memory of meeting someone for the first time?</em></p><p><a href="https://twitter.com/Herrbertling">Markus</a> wrote:</p><blockquote>I really like to remember how I met my current girlfriend.
