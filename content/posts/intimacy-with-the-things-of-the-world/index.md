<p>I thought I'd try an experiment this year. I'm curious what it would be like to spend each week listening to a single album, starting a new one after the week is up.</p><p>I miss that feeling of knowing something completely, returning to it <a href="http://www.robinsloan.com/fish/">again and again</a>, intimately aware of its flaws and perfections. I played a vinyl album so many times growing up that I'm a little disappointed when that one song doesn't skip.</p><p>I hear music all the time, but not in that way that it becomes part of me, when a melody triggers a memory and I anticipate (or even dread) the next song as another ends.</p><p>The book <em>Still Life with Oysters</em> and Lemon by Mark Doty is a mediation on a single painting by the same name. The author writes beautifully about being consumed by one thing.</p><blockquote>I have felt the energy and life of the painting's will; I have been held there, instructed. And the overall effect, the result of looking and looking into its brimming surface as long as I could look, is love, by which I mean a sense of tenderness toward experience, of being held within an intimacy with the things of the world.</blockquote><p>There is great joy in abundance, possibility wrapped in an overflowing buffet of sounds, tastes, and experiences.
