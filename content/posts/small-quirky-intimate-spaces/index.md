<p>We watch a lot of live performances at my house. It can't match experiencing a concert in person, but those opportunities are rare compared to the endless catalog of shows available from YouTube, the <a href="http://www.itunesfestival.com/">iTunes Music Festival</a>, and our hometown favorite, <a href="http://video.pbs.org/program/austin-city-limits/">Austin City Limits</a>. It's a great way to revisit favorites and sample new bands.</p><p>Plus, it's fun to compare stage techniques; how artists react to a subdued crowd or ride the momentum of a lively audience. There is the sing-along and the call-and-response; the exhortations from the stage — "Is there anybody out there?", "Make some noise!", and the reliable, "Let me hear you, city name!". I remember Elvis Costello introducing a song with "Seats are made for standing on!".</p><p>My favorite rock 'n' roll question is Bruce Springsteen's, "<a href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L3YWl0JniGg&amp;feature=youtu.be&amp;t=8m50s">Is there anybody alive out there?</a>"</p><p>When the moment arrives, the concert is already at a peak. The crowd is on top of the world, the noise louder than in the dreams of an average band. All of which makes the question even better. It's an exhortation to the audience and the band; there's something beyond this feeling, higher ground is within our reach. He repeats it again and again, as the response becomes deafening.</p><blockquote>Is there anybody alive out there?&lt;br&gt;Is there anybody alive out there?&lt;br&gt;Is there anybody alive out there?</blockquote><p>He keeps asking until even the most jaded can no longer ignore it.</p><p>I hear that question in my head often, especially when I see mundane routine where I once saw magic, and nitpicking negativity has replaced wide-eyed wonder. It's easy to narrow your focus to the point where you can no longer see what drew you to this person or thing or work in the first place.</p><p><em>For the ones who had a notion, a notion deep inside, that it ain't no sin to be glad you're alive.</em> Whether a community of friends, kind stranger, or aging rock star, I'm grateful for reminders of the privilege that is life.</p><h3 id="prompted">Prompted</h3><p><a href="/uncommon/story/away-from-convention">Last week's dispatch</a> asked, <em>What could a new friend listen to, read, or watch to better understand you?</em></p><p><a href="https://twitter.com/smwat">Sara</a> wrote:</p><blockquote>30 Rock.
