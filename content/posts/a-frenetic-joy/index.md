<p>I read a post the other day.</p><p><em>Checking for updates...</em></p><p>It was about how to improve and be better in work or life, I forget which.</p><p><em>A new version is available.</em></p><p>There were five tips, maybe seven.</p><p><em>This update includes performance improvements and bug fixes.</em></p><p>I love these short collections of advice. There is much to learn and many people eager to offer guidance.</p><p><em>Do you want to install now?</em></p><p>Sometimes, though, it's as if I'm perpetually chasing a new and improved version of myself.</p><p><em>This will require a restart. Are you sure?</em></p><p>If I apply the lessons of others in just the right combination, I will overcome any imperfections. I will be smarter, better, happier, more productive.</p><p><em>Please wait while the update is downloaded.</em></p><p>It's hard to know where the line is between pursuing worthwhile, rewarding, and necessary, growth and seeing shortcomings at every turn.</p><p><em>To restart now, tap Continue.</em></p><p>I suppose it is mostly a matter of balance. It usually is, isn't it?</p><p><em>Restarting…</em></p><p>For me, balance means deciding now and then to stop chasing upgrades.</p><p><em>Here's a quick overview of what's new.</em></p><p>And instead, enjoy the features and imperfections of this version for a little while.</p><p><em>Check for updates?</em></p><h3 id="prompted">Prompted</h3><p><a href="/uncommon/story/small-quirky-intimate-spaces">Last week's dispatch</a> asked, <em>What's your most memorable concert experience?</em></p><p><a href="https://twitter.com/kalachimera">Rachel</a> wrote:</p><blockquote>I once went to an outdoor concert at my college. We should have been miserable as we were getting soaked to the skin, but the excellent music and the ridiculousness of the experience combined to produce a euphoric feeling that seemed to be shared by the rest of the crowd. The focus was fully on the music, and the audience was repeatedly encouraged to join in and sing. It worked.
