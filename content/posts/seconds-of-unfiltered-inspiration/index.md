<p>There's something different about friendships formed across great distance. My theory is the lack of proximity requires more intentional effort for these unique relationships to survive and thrive. That investment makes the moments when you do meet in person and bits become atoms particularly special.</p><p>This morning, I took a breezy walk to a cafe where I finally met <a href="https://twitter.com/lmsanchez">Lisa</a>, a treasured friend and Uncommon contributor.</p><p>Lisa has been part of this community since the very beginning and wrote many of the dispatches during the first year (including <a href="https://bb.place/uncommon/story/striving-to-live-a-life-that-matters">this favorite</a>). Her generous spirit and eloquent words infuse Uncommon, from the warm welcome ("When was the last time you stopped...") to the carefully chosen poems (yes, poems!) on our error pages to "a front porch for the Internet".</p><p>Her contribution has been far beyond words, though. She has been a constant source of wisdom, perspective, and good cheer. Her belief in community and love of people is a refreshing reminder of why Uncommon exists.</p><p>Today's conversation went by so quickly. In our short amount of time, we spoke of many things, sharing stories of brave accomplishment and future trepidation, but we didn't talk about Uncommon. Funny enough, that's often the case when I get to spend time with Uncommon friends, and many of you have shared similar experiences. I love it.</p><p>Communities form around apps and games and shared interests all the time, and sometimes people get together in person and talk about that shared interest. It's wonderful.</p><p>But what's the shared interest of our community? It's a fierce belief in the inherent value of each person and each moment. Wherever people gather, in person or online, in restaurants or homes, that's what we talk about and celebrate. And as it's always been, long before bits bounced about the world, there is wonder and possibility found there.</p><h3 id="prompted">Prompted</h3><p><a href="https://bb.place/uncommon/story/i-could-say-it-all-day">Last week's dispatch</a> asked, <em>Do you have a recipe for inspiration?</em></p><p><a href="https://twitter.com/workjon">Jon</a> wrote:</p><blockquote>I think most people think of finding inspiration like fishing - the fish bite or they don't.
