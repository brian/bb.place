<blockquote>In an old house in Paris that was covered with vines<br>Lived twelve little girls in two straight lines.<br>In two straight lines, they broke their bread,<br>and brushed their teeth, and went to bed.<br>They smiled at the good, and frowned at the bad,<br>and sometimes they were very sad.</blockquote><p>Last week's horrific events in Newtown, Connecticut have been swirling in my mind and heart. I don't think any of us know how to process such a tragedy. Everything seems trivial in the face of such overwhelming sorrow.</p><p>For some reason, my mind keeps drifting to <em>Madeline</em> by Ludwig Bemelmans, one of my favorite children's books. It's the wonderful tale of a school for young girls, including the plucky Madeline and her teacher and protector, Miss Clavel. It's Miss Clavel's keen ability to sense trouble that I return to again and again.</p><blockquote>In the middle of the night, Miss Clavel turned on her light. And she said, "Something is not right.”</blockquote><p><em>Something is not right.</em> We know that now. We likely knew it before, but we were able to lose ourselves in the day-to-day, rushing to the next place and next thing, and not face it.</p><p>There are so many stories of Miss Clavel in Newtown, Connecticut; protectors who fought and shielded, comforted and quieted. <a href="http://www.nytimes.com/2012/12/18/nyregion/amid-the-whiz-of-bullets-seeking-comfort-in-song.html?smid=tw-share">This New York Times story</a> is unbelievably gripping. There's a line in that piece about the children at Sandy Hook Elementary School that I will never forget: "...it was hard to run with their eyes closed."</p><p>We know, little ones. We know because we've been doing it for a long time.</p><p>I can't offer a solution. I only know that things will get better through thousands of small decisions by every one of us, and maybe a few big ones, too. For the children in our midst, and their protectors, let's stop running, and open our eyes.</p>
