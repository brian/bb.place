<p>I hope your week is off to a wonderful start. The last few days have been filled<br>with delightful conversations about this idea. Your responses to the last email<br>were fascinating. We share many wishes for this community, but each of you has<br>your own clever twists on how to turn those wishes into something real. I have<br>no doubt that we’ll do just that.</p><h3 id="your-thoughts">Your thoughts</h3><p>I wanted to share some of the thoughtful replies you sent this week. These were<br>answers to the question, <em>What would you most like to get out of a community like</em><br><em>this?</em></p><blockquote>A deeper, meaningful connection with people, not just random statuses,<br>or thoughts thrown out, but interesting conversations where the<br>takeaways continue on with you for minutes, hours, or days after<br>you have walked away. I am tired of internet noise. I love when I<br>talk to my friends on the phone, this is because the conversation we<br>share plays on in my thoughts for a great deal of time after we have<br>hung-up the phone. One of my favorite activities to share with my<br>closest friends is sitting around a fire, the conversation and ideas<br>just flow, you learn things about your friends and you learn things<br>about yourself. It’s beautiful to sit back and watch unfold. —<br><a href="https://twitter.com/topsurf">Patty</a></blockquote><blockquote>I’d like this to help me think deeper, develop insight and wisdom<br>(instead of just collecting information like social media does now)<br>and understand more about myself, other people, and what makes the<br>world work. — <a href="https://twitter.com/granthensel">Grant</a></blockquote><blockquote>I ask lots of questions (and enjoy answering them), so it would be<br>great to participate in a space that encouraged exchange like that --<br>pretty simple, that’s it :-) — <a href="https://twitter.com/semil">Semil</a></blockquote><h3 id="a-new-name">A new name</h3><p>Our undertaking now has a new name! Since the first blog post, a few people who<br>love the idea have mentioned that they didn’t love the name. The more I<br>listened, the more I agreed with them. Though Toyed With has some of the fun,<br>whimsy and embracing of new ideas that I was looking for, it feels temporary and<br>experimental, which is almost the opposite of my intentions. My hope is that<br>this community will last a long time, and that what we’re sharing with each<br>other, including our favorite things in the world, is meaningful and worthy of<br>our attention.</p><p>So, we came up with a name that hopefully captures the spirit of this project<br>much better, <strong>Uncommon in Common</strong>. I hope you love it. You can<br><a href="https://twitter.com/uncommon">follow @uncommon on Twitter</a> right now and sometime soon, uncommon.cc will<br>be a thing (but not yet, trust me). Though I expect <em>Uncommon</em> will quickly<br>become the way we talk about this, we’ll use uncommonincommon dot com now and<br>then, too, because it’s quite possibly the best domain name in the world :)</p><h3 id="uncommon-reads">Uncommon reads</h3><p><a href="http://www.hunterwalk.com/2012/01/sorry-mike-facebook-could-reboot-and.html">Hunter Walker wrote a post</a> earlier this year about what we see happen to online communities time after time. Semil mentioned it recently and if you’re interested in helping this new community take root and truly be uncommon, please<br>take the time to. I’d love to hear what it says to you.</p><blockquote>I believe most social communication products - from Facebook to<br>Twitter to Path are designed in a way which causes us to all slowly<br>ruin the quality of our experience. Basically we say we love high<br>signal, low noise services but then proceed to bring the noise.</blockquote><h3 id="your-turn">Your turn</h3><p>Finally, a few things for you...</p><ol><li>Do you have too much great stuff to read, watch, and listen to and not<br>enough time, or do you wish you had better sources of high-quality content?</li><li>Do you have skills and time you’d like to contribute to this endeavor? If<br>you do, let me know. The focus at the moment is on web and mobile<br>development. If you happen to be a talented designer or know one, the sooner<br>the better :)</li><li>Keep this question in the back of your mind and I’ll ask about it next time:<br><em>What’s the favorite thing you’ve heard this week?</em></li></ol><p>Until we have a place of our own, feel free to introduce yourself to any of the folks I mention here or browse the fine people <a href="https://twitter.com/uncommon/following">@uncommon follows</a>. The community and conversation have already started.</p>
