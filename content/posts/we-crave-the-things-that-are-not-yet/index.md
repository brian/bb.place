<p>I finally started watching <em>House of Cards</em>, the Kevin Spacey/David Fincher show about Washington politics on Netflix. I'm sure many of you have already watched the 13 episodes. I know it was released in February, but I'm running behind.</p><p><em>Finally</em> is an interesting word, isn't it? For me, it captures this moment in time perfectly.</p><p>Finally is everywhere. We talk about finally visiting a new restaurant, finishing the book everyone is talking about, seeing the summer blockbuster, and getting caught up. We use finally for watching shows that are only months old and trying apps that came out last week.</p><p>It's just a word, of course, but I think it's telling. So much of our culture and social media feeds that sense that we are always terribly behind. There's excitement about the latest thing, but with it the underlying fear of missing out.</p><p>I feel like finally used to be saved for really big things. <em>I finally visited Paris. I finally asked him out. I finally landed the job. I finally graduated. I finally learned to cook.</em></p><p>What's interesting to me is that sometimes we let others decide what our finally should be and sometimes we decide for ourselves.</p><p>I'd like to repurpose finally. I want to see if I can step off the consumption treadmill and set my own pace. I want to finally lose track of time and finally forget what's on my to-do list. Maybe I'll finally ask, "Are you doing okay?" and one day, finally write a novel.</p><p>In the meantime, I hope to be fully present in the here and now.
